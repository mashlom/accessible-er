# Shop: paper-doll colouring sheets, brief for the artist

With the stars earned at the stations, the child "buys" items in the world's shop. Afterwards the family gets an
**A4 PDF** by email: a black-and-white paper doll of the world's character and the bought items, to **colour in**,
cut out with scissors and play with at home.

- Each world has **8 items**; the child picks **4**.
- One A4 sheet holds **the doll + the 4 bought items** (e.g. the knight with a helmet, chain mail, armour and a
  shield). The items fit onto the doll with small fold-over tabs.
- The dinosaurs have no doll: the 8 items are dinosaurs that stand on their own (a fold-back stand).

**What to deliver, per world:** the doll and its 8 items, each piece a separate file, named as in the tables
(`shop-<world>-doll`, `shop-<world>-<item>`), PNG with a transparent background at 300 dpi, or SVG.

## Rules (for every piece)

- **Black line art only**: bold, even outlines (about 1.5 mm when printed), white inside; no shading, no grey, no
  colour, no background, no text.
- **Easy to colour and to cut**: friendly rounded shapes like the app's worlds, simple inner details, no tiny areas,
  a clear outer outline, a little space around each piece.
- **The doll**: a child, standing straight, front view, arms slightly away from the body, in plain base clothes,
  about **18 cm** tall on A4. All dolls the same size, so the sheets match.
- **The items**: front view, at the doll's scale, so each one fits onto the doll exactly; small fold-over tabs at the
  shoulders, waist or sides.
- **Nothing sharp or scary**: no weapons (no swords or spears), nothing medical that frightens.

## Prompt

Use this at the start of every prompt, then add the piece from the table:

> Black-and-white colouring-page paper-doll set for a children's hospital app, printed on A4 at home, coloured in
> with crayons and cut out with scissors. Clean black line art only: bold, even outlines (about 1.5 mm when printed),
> white inside, no shading, no grey, no colour, no background, no text. Friendly rounded shapes, simple inner details
> that are easy to colour, no tiny areas. The doll stands straight, front view, arms slightly away from the body, in
> plain base clothes, about 18 cm tall on A4. Each item is drawn front view at the doll's scale, so it fits onto the
> doll exactly, with small fold-over tabs at the shoulders, waist or sides. Each piece is separate, with a little
> space around it for cutting. Nothing sharp or scary, no weapons.

## The A4 sheet

The doll on one side, the 4 bought items around it, dashed cutting lines, the world's name and a small "colour, cut
out and play" line; black and white only, generous margins for home printers. The app puts the sheet together from
the pieces.

## Before sending

- Print a test sheet on a home printer: the lines are clear, nothing is too small to cut.
- Every item fits onto its doll.
- The team (MLR) looks at the pieces before they go in, as with the other pictures.

## Redo: one scale, and tabs that fold along the edge

The first set cannot be used as paper dolls: every piece was drawn to fill its own canvas, so the pieces are not at
the doll's scale (the space helmet came out more than twice as wide as the doll, the mission badge as big as its
body), and nothing fits on the doll without guessing. The tabs also come off too easily. Please redraw the pieces
with the changes below, and redraw the dolls too (section 5).

### 1. One canvas and one scale per world

- **Every piece of a world is drawn on a canvas exactly the size of that world's doll picture** (the same width and
  height in pixels as `shop-<world>-doll`), transparent everywhere else.
- **Each piece stands where it goes on the doll**, at the doll's scale: the helmet or hat exactly over the doll's
  head, the suit, dress or armour over its body, the boots or shoes over its feet, the gloves over its hands. Laid on
  top of the doll picture, the piece fits it exactly, like a transparent layer.
- **Things held in the hand** (flag, banner, wand, lantern, torch, camera, basket, map, bottle) stand at the doll's
  hand, in the size they would really have next to a child.
- **Nothing is stretched to fill the canvas.** A small piece (a badge, a pair of gloves) stays small.
- The app then prints the doll and its pieces on a landscape A4 sheet at **one and the same reduction**: the doll
  **18 cm tall**, so it fits on one half of the sheet (A5, 148 x 210 mm), the pieces beside it (they may use the free
  part of the doll's half, never covering the doll); what does not fit goes on a second page at the same scale.
- The dinosaurs have no doll and are **not** at one scale (the brachiosaurus would fill the sheet): each one is drawn
  to fill its own canvas, as now, and printed at a size that suits it.
- The safari explorer's doll is the same as the diver's (bare feet / socks, so the boots go on it); the explorer gets
  trousers as a piece.

### 2. Tabs fold along the edge of the picture, no fold lines

- **No dashed or drawn fold lines** on the tabs.
- **A tab folds along the edge of the piece itself**: it is the piece's outline that is folded, so the tab is a flap
  that continues the piece's own edge and bends back over the doll's edge. For example, on a dress the shoulder tabs
  continue the top of the straps upwards and fold down over the doll's shoulders (top to bottom), the side tabs
  continue the waist sideways and fold back around the doll's sides.
- A tab joins the piece **along its whole base**, on a straight stretch of the piece's edge, at least 6 mm
  long when printed, never through a thin neck, so it does not tear off when it is folded. It does not have to run
  the whole edge: a side tab can be shorter than the side it sits on.

### 3. Shoes and boots: a slit for the doll's foot

- A pair of shoes or boots is not a flat picture laid over the feet: each shoe has a **slit across its top opening**
  (along the line where the foot goes in), so the doll's foot slides into it.
- Draw the shoe from the front as usual (with its whole opening), the slit marked as a single cut line (not a fold
  line), no tabs. Place the slit so that once the doll's foot is pushed through it, the shoe's back part is hidden
  behind the leg and the lower leg looks as if it is inside the shoe: the doll really seems to be wearing it.

### 4. Hats and helmets: tabs where they hold the head

- The tabs of a hat, a helmet or a crown sit **at the sides of the doll's head, at the height of its ears / temples**,
  pointing sideways and folding back behind the head, so that together they grip the head. Not on top of the hat and
  not at its brim's outer ends far from the head.
- Check on the doll: with the hat in place, the tabs' fold lines (the hat's own edge) touch the head's outline on both
  sides.

### 5. The doll itself

- **Base clothes, minimal:** a plain sleeveless top (no sleeves, no collar, no belt, no pockets or prints) and plain
  shorts or briefs; on the feet **socks at most, never shoes or boots** (the shoes are pieces to put on).
- **Hands made to hold things:** each hand is a small fist with a **slit** through it (one cut line), into which a
  stick or a handle slides. Draw the hands so that they can hold every held piece of the world.

### 6. Pieces held in the hand: think how the hand holds them

Every held piece is made to work with the doll's fist, not just laid next to it:

- **Sticks** (a wand, a staff, a banner or flag pole, a toy horse's stick, a torch's handle): a long, straight, even
  part that slides through the slit in the fist and stays there.
- **Things with a handle** (a lantern, a bag, a basket, a net bag): the handle is drawn as a closed loop or a straight
  bar that slides into the slit, so the thing hangs from the fist.
- **Other small things** (binoculars, a camera, a map, a bottle): a strap or a stick to hold them by, made the same
  way.
- Check on the doll: every held piece goes into a fist and stays there, at its natural place in the hand.

### 7. Wings and other things worn on the back

- **Wings** (and a backpack, a jet pack, an air tank) are worn **behind the doll**: the piece itself stays at the
  doll's back, and instead of tabs it has **two straps that go over the doll's shoulders and hang down in front**,
  like the front panels of a waistcoat. The straps join the piece along a straight stretch of its edge (at least
  6 mm) and fold over the shoulders along that edge.
- From the front, the doll is seen with the wings (or the pack) rising behind it and the two straps on its chest.

### Prompt (add to the shared prompt above, instead of its line about the tabs)

### Before sending

- Lay every piece over its doll: it sits exactly on the right place, at the right size.
- No fold lines anywhere; every tab joins the piece along a straight stretch of its edge (at least 6 mm).
- Every shoe has its slit and no tabs; the doll's foot fits through the slit.
- On every hat, helmet and crown the tabs reach the sides of the doll's head.
- Every held piece slides into the doll's fist; wings and packs stay behind the doll, their straps over its
  shoulders.
- The doll wears only a plain sleeveless top, shorts and socks; both hands are fists with slits.
